1. **Pre-IPO perps** track the **expected public market price** of a company.
2. When the IPO launches, the **contract price converges** toward the actual stock price.
3. If the convergence is **mispriced**, leveraged traders face **liquidation risk**.

In Unitree’s case, the **$9.1 million in open interest** and **$59 million in turnover** on Hyperliquid suggest **institutional participation**, with traders betting that:

- **Unitree’s humanoid robot shipments (5,500+ units)** will justify a **multi-billion-dollar valuation**.
- **The IPO will open at a premium**, allowing early traders to **cash out at 4x**.

However, this **speculative bubble** is not without risk. The **convergence mechanism**—where the perpetual price aligns with the IPO price—is **not guaranteed**. If Unitree’s stock **underperforms**, traders could face **mass liquidations**, as seen in past cases like **CXMT (a Chinese memory-chip maker)**, where the pre-IPO price was **within 2.5% of its opening price**.
