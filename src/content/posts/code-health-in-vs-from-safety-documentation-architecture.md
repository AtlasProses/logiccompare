# The Core Engineering Reality & Metric Baselines

In recent years, Large Language Models (LLMs) have become increasingly prominent in software engineering, particularly in code health and safety documentation. Two recent studies, "Code Health in LLM-Based Test Generation" and "From Safety Documentation to Safety Knowledge Support," have shed light on the effectiveness and limitations of LLMs in these areas. In this article, we will examine the architectural trade-offs and failure modes of these two approaches.

**Raw Data & Metric Summary**

The "Code Health in LLM-Based Test Generation" study found that code health, as measured by CodeScene's CodeHealth (CH), provides a weak but consistent signal of LLM-generated test effectiveness. The study also found that CH is negatively correlated with input-token count. Specifically, the study reported a correlation coefficient of -0.23 between CH and input-token count.

In contrast, the "From Safety Documentation to Safety Knowledge Support" study proposed an evidence-grounded framework for safety knowledge support in medical devices. The framework connects device artifacts, controlled knowledge storage and retrieval, method-specific generation of candidate safety items, critique and uncertainty checks, and recorded expert review. The study reported a coverage rate of 92% for safety artifacts generated by the framework.
