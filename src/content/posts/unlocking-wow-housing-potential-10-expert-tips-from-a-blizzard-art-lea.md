**Establishing a Reference Image: The Foundation of WoW Housing Design**

According to Jay Huang, Blizzard's principal artist and lead designer, finding a reference image is crucial for getting started with WoW housing design. A reference image serves as a visual guide, helping designers to determine the overall aesthetic and layout of their in-game home.

When selecting a reference image, consider the style and theme you want to achieve. Look for inspiration in real-world architecture, art, or even other WoW housing designs. A reference image will help you to identify the key elements that make up the design, including the use of colors, textures, and spatial arrangements.

**Spatial Planning: Breaking Down the Space**

One of the most significant challenges in WoW housing design is spatial planning. With the game's vast array of decor items and furniture, it can be overwhelming to decide where to start. Huang recommends breaking down the space into smaller, manageable areas.

Use partitions, pillars, and platforms to create separate zones within your in-game home. This will help to define different areas of the space, making it easier to decorate and furnish. By breaking down the space, you can create a more cohesive and functional design.

**Color Palettes: Limiting Colors for a Cohesive Look**

When it comes to color palettes, Huang suggests limiting yourself to 2-4 colors maximum. This will help to create a cohesive look and prevent the space from feeling overwhelming.

Choose colors that complement each other and fit with the overall theme of your design. Consider the 60-30-10 rule, where 60% of the space is dominated by a primary color, 30% by a secondary color, and 10% by an accent color.

**Repetition and Patterns: Adding Depth to Your Design**

Repetition and patterns can add depth and visual interest to your WoW housing design. Huang recommends using repeating elements, such as pillars or arches, to create a sense of rhythm and harmony.

Patterns can also be used to add texture and visual interest to your design. Consider using rugs, tapestries, or other decorative items to create a sense of depth and dimensionality.
