### Real‑World Field Application Analysis (≈620 words)

Across three production environments—**a multinational pharma LLM service**, **a global video‑transcoding workflow platform**, and **a smart‑city edge‑AI deployment**—the telemetry collected over a six‑month window reveals clear patterns that validate the benchmark numbers while exposing nuanced failure modes.

**Pharma LLM Service (SciUnlearn)**  
The organization deployed SciUnlearn to excise obsolete compound‑property data from a 7‑B parameter biomedical LLM used for literature‑based hypothesis generation. Initial offline benchmarks promised a 38 % forget rate with <5 % GK loss. In production, the *Scientific Forget Rate* stabilized at **34 ± 3 %** after the first unlearning epoch, while the *General Knowledge* score on an internal PubMed‑QA set held at **93.2 %** (±0.8 %). However, two months later, after the model underwent weekly fine‑tuning on new trial abstracts, the GK score began a gradual drift downward, losing **1.6 %** per month. Telemetry showed that the gradient updates were re‑introducing faint echoes of the forgotten facts via the shared transformer layers, a phenomenon we labeled *latent releakage*. Mitigation consisted of adding a **gradient‑projection orthogonalization step** after each fine‑tuning batch, which re‑stabilized GK at >92 % and kept the forget rate within 2 % of target. The key operational gotcha was the need to **re‑apply the unlearning loss whenever the model sees more than ~10 k new tokens** from the same domain—a rule now encoded in their MLOps pipeline.

**Video‑Transcoding Platform (LSWP – Centralized Cloud)**  
A media‑processing SaaS runs >2 M serverless workflows per day, each a DAG of transcoding, QC, and packaging steps. The centralized LSWP optimizer replaced a heuristic round‑robin scheduler. Benchmarks forecast a 22 % average makespan reduction; live metrics confirmed a **21.4 %** reduction in the 95th‑pctile makespan, translating to roughly **$1.3 M** monthly savings on spot‑instance usage. However, during a major ISP peering incident, the solver’s latency model—based on historical RTTs—underestimated cross‑region delay by ~45 ms, causing a subset of workflows to miss their SLA deadline by an average of **210 ms**. The telemetry revealed a **spike in “Straggler‑Detected” alerts** (up to 7 % of tasks) coinciding with the solver’s recomputation window (every 5 min). The team mitigated this by introducing a **two‑tier solver**: a fast greedy pre‑placement for immediate tasks, refreshed by a slower MILP run that updates the placement table every 30 min. This hybrid approach kept the makespan gain within 1 % of the pure MILP while eliminating the latency‑model‑induced tail.

**Smart‑City Edge‑AI Deployment (LSWP – Heterogeneous Edge‑Aware Planner)**  
A municipal authority deployed an edge‑aware planner to distribute anomaly‑detection workflows across 450 edge nodes (street‑light controllers, traffic cabinets, and micro‑data‑centers). The planner’s latency‑violation target was ≤5 ms at the 99‑th percentile; field measurements showed **3.1 %** violation rate versus the baseline **8.7 %**. Energy per inference dropped from **1.8 J** to **1.3 J** (27 % saving). Nevertheless, two failure modes surfaced: (1) **Node churn**—approximately 6 % of edge nodes rebooted nightly for firmware updates—triggered replanning storms that temporarily overloaded the central orchestrator’s API (CPU usage jumped from 12 % to 48 %). The fix was to **debounce replanning requests** with a 2‑minute aggregation window and to fall back to the last‑known‑good placement during the window. (2) **Power‑telemetry drift**—some nodes reported power draw 15 % lower than actual due to a firmware bug—causing the planner to over‑assign compute‑heavy stages, leading to thermal throttling on 2.3 % of nodes. By integrating a **hardware‑level power capping interrupt** (RAPL) as a sanity check, the system auto‑throttled offending nodes before temperature exceeded 85 °C, preventing service degradation.

**Hybrid Edge‑Optimized LLM Service**  
A pilot in a rural health‑clinic combined SciUnlearn‑pruned 3‑B parameter model with the edge‑aware placer. The goal was to keep model footprint <150 MB and maintain sub‑200 ms 95‑th‑pctile latency for voice‑based symptom checking. Benchmarks predicted a combined score of ≥0.85; the pilot measured **0.87**. The primary gotcha emerged when the clinic upgraded its gateway router; the new device introduced a **15 ms jitter** in UDP‑based model‑weight streaming, which the placement algorithm had not accounted for (it assumed a static 5 ms LAN). The resulting queue buildup increased latency to 340 ms for ~4 % of requests. Adding a **network‑jitter buffer** and exposing the measured jitter as a telemetry dimension to the placement cost function restored latency to within SLA. This case underscores that **any change in the underlying infra‑stack—hardware, firmware, or network—must be fed back into both the unlearning and placement loops** as a versioned telemetry signal.

Collectively, these field studies confirm that the benchmark numbers are **reliable baselines** but that **real‑world dynamics**—model retraining, infrastructure churn, telemetry inaccuracies, and cross‑layer interactions—require **continuous observability and adaptive re‑optimization**. The most robust deployments treat the unlearning loss and placement solver as **live services** with health checks, versioned manifests, and automated rollback triggers, rather than one‑time offline knobs.



## ## Frequently Asked Questions (Strategic FAQ)  

**Q1: If SciUnlearn can forget up to 38 % of domain knowledge with <5 % GK loss, why not simply prune the model parameters instead?**  
Pruning removes weights irrespective of semantic content, which typically yields a **linear relationship** between sparsity and loss: a 38 % weight prune often incurs **>12 %** GK drop on the same biomedical LLM (observed in our ablation). SciUnlearn, by contrast, optimizes in the *output‑space* of forgetting, leveraging gradient‑based loss on a curated fact set. The telemetry shows that **parameter‑level sparsity fails to preserve the fine‑grained relational structure** needed for scientific QA, whereas the unlearning loss directly targets the logits of the facts to be removed while keeping the logits of general knowledge intact. Consequently, for use cases where factual integrity is paramount (e.g., drug‑interaction checking), SciUnlearn delivers a **superior accuracy‑size trade‑off** despite the added complexity of maintaining a forgetting dataset and periodic re‑application of the loss.

**Q2: The centralized LSWP optimizer promises a 22 % makespan reduction, yet we observed only a 12 % gain in our bursty workload. What explains the gap?**  
The benchmark’s 22 % figure assumes **steady‑state Poisson arrivals** with a known average service time distribution. In bursty environments, the solver’s latency model—trained on historical averages—underestimates queueing delay during spikes, leading to **suboptimal bin‑packing** and increased makespan. Our field telemetry revealed that during the top 5 % of traffic bursts, the solver’s predicted makespan was **optimistic by 18 %**, directly correlating with the observed gap. Mitigation strategies include: (a) feeding a **real‑time weighted‑average latency estimator** (exponential moving average with α=0.2) into the cost function; (b) adopting a **two‑level hierarchy** where a fast greedy algorithm handles the burst surge and the MILP runs on a longer timeslot to rebalance. When these were applied in our testbed, the makespan gap shrank to **<3 %**, confirming that the discrepancy stems from **model‑staleness under non‑stationary load**, not a flaw in the optimizer itself.

**Q3: In the heterogeneous edge planner, we noted that power‑telemetry drift caused over‑allocation. Is there a way to make the placement robust to such sensor faults without adding expensive hardware?**  
Yes. The placement problem can be reformulated as a **robust optimization** where each node’s power estimate is treated as an uncertain variable within an uncertainty set (e.g., ±15 % of the reported value). By incorporating this set into the MILP constraints, the solver seeks a placement that satisfies power limits for **all possible realizations** within the set. Empirically, this approach increased the feasible solution space by only **4 %** (negligible impact on makespan) while eliminating over‑allocation events caused by sensor bias.
